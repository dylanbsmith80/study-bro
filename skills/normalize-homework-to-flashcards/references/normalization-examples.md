# Normalization Examples

Use these examples as decision patterns, not as facts to copy into unrelated decks.

## Completed study guide: preserve

Source:

- **Mitosis:** Cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells.

Card:

- Term: `Mitosis`
- Definition: `Cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells.`

Keep the source wording because it is already concise, self-contained, and deck-ready.

## Homework and multipart prompts: normalize

Source:

> Question 5. (a) State the Lorentz force law. (b) In problem 5f, calculate the force on a proton when B points in the x direction.

Cards:

- Term: `How do you calculate the force on a charged particle in an electromagnetic wave?`
- Definition: `Use the Lorentz force, F = q(E + v × B). Calculate the electric and magnetic contributions as vectors before adding them.`

Optionally add a worked example only when the source supplies every required vector, value, unit, and assumption. Do not preserve “Question 5,” “part b,” or “problem 5f.”

## Mixed document: classify by section

Source:

- Notes section: `Inflation is a general rise in prices and a decline in purchasing power.`
- Practice section: `Problem 3: Using the graph above, explain what happened to purchasing power.`

Cards:

- Preserve the notes as an `Inflation` definition card with light cleanup.
- Normalize the practice prompt into a standalone relationship card only if the graph or source text supports the answer. Do not rewrite the entire notes section as question cards.

## Numerical problem: method plus optional example

Source:

> Problem 8: A 2 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s². Find the net force.

Method card:

- Term: `How is net force calculated from mass and acceleration?`
- Definition: `Apply Newton’s second law, F_net = ma. Use mass in kilograms and acceleration in meters per second squared to obtain force in newtons.`

Optional example card:

- Term: `What is the net force on a 2 kg object accelerating at 3 m/s²?`
- Definition: `F_net = ma = (2 kg)(3 m/s²) = 6 N.`

Keep the example because it is fully specified and illustrates a reusable calculation.

## Diagram-dependent prompt: describe or review

Source:

> Part B: Using the diagram below, determine the magnetic-field direction at point P.

Normalize only when the visible source establishes the geometry. Describe the relevant orientation in the card itself, and attach the original diagram only when the Study Bro image-association rules approve it. If point P or the geometry is unavailable, omit the card or mark it for review. Never invent the diagram.

## Ambiguous prompt: do not invent

Source:

> Question 12: Calculate the final velocity using the values from the previous question.

Result:

- Do not create a calculation card when the initial velocity, acceleration, time, or governing context is missing.
- A general kinematics-method card is acceptable only when that method is clearly the knowledge being tested and is grounded by the surrounding source.
- Otherwise add the item to the external review list as an uncertain interpretation.
